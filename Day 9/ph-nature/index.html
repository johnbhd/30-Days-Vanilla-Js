Why the Philippines

A country that refuses to be just one thing.

You can snorkel a coral wall before lunch, walk through a rice terrace older than most nations after it, then eat grilled fish on a beach where the only light is a lantern. The archipelago runs from Batanes' wind-scoured grass hills in the north to Tawi-Tawi's reefs in the south, and almost none of it looks the same twice.

It is also, quietly, one of the cheapest and warmest places in Asia to travel slowly. Ferries run late. Someone will always hand you a plate.

Where to go

Six places to start.

You will not see them all. That is the point. Pick two, stay longer than you planned.

Palawan · Mimaropa

El Nido

Bacuit Bay's karst towers, hidden lagoons and sandbars that appear only at low tide. Sleep in town, spend the days on a bangka.

Best Nov–May4–6 days

Bohol · Visayas

Chocolate Hills

1,200 perfect cones that turn cocoa-brown in the dry season.

Best Dec–May2 days

Ifugao · Cordillera

Batad Terraces

Two thousand years of hand-carved steps, still farmed today.

Best Feb–May3 days

Siargao · Surigao del Norte

Siargao

Cloud 9 surf, palm-lined backroads and lagoons the colour of glass.

Best Mar–Oct5 days

Albay · Bicol

Mayon

The world's most symmetrical volcano, with a town at its foot.

Best Jan–May2 days

Sulu Sea · Palawan

Tubbataha Reefs

A UNESCO marine park reachable only by liveaboard, and worth it.

Best Mar–Jun6 days

Ways to lose a week

Four ways to disappear.

  • Island hopping

    Six lagoons before lunch, a sandbar at golden hour. Bring a dry bag and no plan.

  • Reef diving

    Thresher sharks at Malapascua, walls at Apo, and the coral cathedral of Tubbataha.

  • Volcano treks

    Sunrise above the clouds on Pulag, or a slow look at Mayon from the rice fields.

  • Culture & kitchens

    Kalinga hand-tattoos, Ifugao harvest songs, and lechon worth the flight alone.
